THE DESIGN PHILOSOPHY

FORM FOLLOWS FEELING.

At Porsche, design is never decorative indulgence. Every curve, taper, and vent must earn its existence through thermodynamic necessity or emotional resonance.

The Unmistakable Flyline

From the very first 356 in 1948 to the latest 992, the gently sloping roofline—the Porsche flyline—instantly signals low aerodynamic resistance and rearward mass concentration. It is the visual DNA of Zuffenhausen.

Sculptural Functionalism

Broad rear shoulders anchor the car visually and physically over wide performance rubber. Air curtains around the front wheels calm turbulent wake, turning aesthetic muscularity into high-speed downforce.

Ergonomic Intuition

Inside, the driver remains the supreme focal point. The central rev counter or digital equivalent aligns directly along the driver’s natural sightline. The ignition switch remains situated on the left—a direct nod to the historic Le Mans running start.

“A product that is coherent in form requires no embellishment; it is enhanced simply by its form.”

— Professor Ferdinand Alexander “Butzi” Porsche, Designer of the 911
THE SEAL OF STUTTGART • EST. 1952

Heraldry of Performance

Designed in 1952 by Ferry Porsche and Franz Xaver Reimspieß, the Porsche Crest marries the historical coat of arms of the Free People’s State of Württemberg—the black and red state stripes and antler horns—with the rearing black steed of Stuttgart, honoring our unbroken roots in Zuffenhausen.

1948 THE BIRTH OF THE BRAND

Porsche 356 "No. 1" Roadster

“In the beginning I looked around and could not find quite the car I was dreaming of. So I decided to build it myself.” — Ferry Porsche

On June 8, 1948, the Porsche 356 “No. 1” Roadster received its general operating permit in Carinthia, Austria. Hand-beaten out of lightweight aluminum over a tubular spaceframe with a mid-mounted air-cooled flat-four engine, it weighed merely 585 kilograms. It established Porsche's founding mantra: performance achieved through agility and lightness rather than sheer mass.

Historical Significance: The very first sports car bearing the official Porsche nameplate.

AERODYNAMIC DEVELOPMENT

Porsche Active Aerodynamics (PAA)

Aerodynamics is the art of balancing high top-speed efficiency with downforce in cornering. PAA uses continuously variable cooling air flaps in the front apron, an extendable rear spoiler with multiple deployment angles, and an airbrake function that shortens braking distances from high speeds.

  • Swan-Neck Wing: Generates downforce on the lower surface with uninterrupted airflow.
  • Active Air Flaps: Close at high speed to lower drag coefficient (\(c_d\)) down to 0.25.
  • Downforce at 285 km/h: Up to 860 kg on GT-series competition models.
